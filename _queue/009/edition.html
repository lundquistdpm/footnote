Pick the meeting by the problem

Seven meetings between now and March, one filter for choosing them, and where I will be talking AI in Saint Paul.

For most of my career I picked meetings by the faculty list and, if I am honest, the forecast. Orlando in March has never been a hard sell for a Minnesotan.

This winter calls for a different filter. These are the first meetings since CMS proposed paying the lower-valued service at half when a visit and a global procedure land on the same day, and the first full season under flat-rate skin substitute payment. The final fee schedule is expected in early November. By the time most of us walk into an exhibit hall, the rules will be written.

My filter this year is simple. Name the problem that cost the most in the last twelve months, and go where that problem is being worked on. A coding day, a limb salvage track, a practice management weekend, a cadaver lab. Each one has a meeting that does it best, and the calendar below is sorted with that in mind.

The CECH will come either way. What an on-demand module cannot give you is the surgeon two seats over who already solved your problem and will walk you through it over coffee. People will help you if you ask.

The podiatrists who come out of 2027 stronger will be the ones who treat conference season as part of the work.

Saint Paul: the Twin Cities Foot & Ankle Conference

The Minnesota Podiatric Medical Association brings its three-day scientific meeting back to the Saint Paul RiverCentre November 12 through 14, with 20 CECH on the table. It draws more than 200 podiatric physicians and surgeons from Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois, Iowa and the Dakotas, with hands-on workshops and labs alongside the lectures.

This is my home meeting, and I will be on the program again talking about AI. Last year the question in the room was whether to use it at all. This year the question is how to supervise it. I will walk through the framework I teach: delegate the work, verify what comes back, disclose that you used it, and own the result. It is the same way we have always run residents and staff.

Two details worth knowing. MPMA membership includes complimentary attendance, and students register free. A student shadowing in your clinic this fall has no reason to miss it.

The regional meeting is where you meet the people you will refer to and take call with for the next twenty years, and that has always been worth the drive.

The rest of the season

AAPPM Annual Conference, Fort Lauderdale, Nov 12–14

The American Academy of Podiatric Practice Management meets the same weekend as Saint Paul, which forces a choice. AAPPM is built for the business side: coding, staffing, operations, and the people who run the front desk. If next year’s fee schedule lands the way it was proposed, the practices that bring their office manager will hear about it first.

Desert Foot, Phoenix, Dec 9–12

Desert Foot is the limb salvage and wound care meeting, and this year it carries extra weight. Medicare moved most skin substitutes to a flat rate this January, and the graft is no longer the business. Debridement, offloading, perfusion and a patient who shows up every week are the treatment, and that is the conversation this meeting was built for.

NY27, New York, Jan 21–24

New York is the volume meeting: a two-floor exhibit hall at the Marriott Marquis and a program that has long opened with a coding day. NY27 lands three weeks after the 2027 rules take effect, which makes it the first big room where podiatry sees real claims under the new fee schedule.

FPMA SAM, Orlando, Jan 27–31

The Florida Podiatric Medical Association’s Science and Management Symposium pairs clinical lectures with practice management in a single week. It is a strong pick for a partner and an associate who want to split the tracks and compare notes on the flight home.

Midwest Foot & Ankle Conference, Chicago, Mar 4–7

The meeting formerly known as the Midwest Podiatry Conference is a partnership of eleven APMA state components, and it sits one week before ACFAS. For Midwest podiatrists who can only take one trip this spring, it is the closer, cheaper one with the people you already refer to.

Orlando: ACFAS 2027

The season ends where it always does for foot and ankle surgeons. The American College of Foot and Ankle Surgeons holds its 85th Annual Scientific Conference at the Orange County Convention Center, with pre-conference programs Thursday, March 11, and the main meeting Friday through Monday, March 12 to 15.

Nearly 2,300 surgeons came to Las Vegas for ACFAS 2026, and the College bills the meeting as the largest foot and ankle gathering in the world. It is where the surgical side of this profession sets its standard: the techniques we argue about, the fixation we trust, the data we will cite for the next five years.

Orlando will feel different from Las Vegas. The 2027 fee schedule will be ten weeks old, AI will be a bigger part of the conversation than it was a year ago, and every surgeon in the building will be doing the math on the same-day visit. None of that makes the meeting less important. It makes the hallway more important.

I will be in Orlando talking about AI, picking up where Saint Paul leaves off. The surgeons who walk into that convention center already knowing what problem they came to solve will leave with the answer.
